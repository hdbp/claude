# Writing Results the way Art writes them

Derived from close reading of the 13 last-author Skoultchi papers 2007–2020 (Nature 2020 Willcockson; Science 2013 Lu; PNAS 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020; Genes Dev 2009; Epigenetics & Chromatin 2014; JBC 2009, 2016; PLoS Genet 2011; Mol Cancer Res 2007; Cell Cycle 2010). The voice is the same across 13 years and two fields: measured, declarative, logic-first, and almost entirely free of ornament. Every paragraph is an argument with a single conclusion; every sentence earns its place by moving the argument one step.

When the user asks for Results text, produce the text, not a commentary on it. When asked to critique, cite the specific rule being violated.

## 1. Macro-architecture

**Subsection headings are claims, not topics.** Write the finding as a complete declarative sentence, present tense, with the agent and the effect named. Keep them 6–16 words.
- "H1 Is Required for Transcriptional Repression of Several Classes of Repetitive Sequences."
- "PU.1 Directly Represses Expression of GATA-1, SCL, Klf1, and Important GATA-1 Cofactors."
- "The C-terminal Domain of H1 Is Essential for Drosophila Development."
- "Forced Expression of Runx1 Is Unable to Block Terminal Erythroid Differentiation in Mice Lacking the Pu.1 URE."
Only method-establishing sections get a neutral heading ("Linker histone H1 is essential for Drosophila"; "Chromatin Accessibility of the Pu.1 Locus Changes during Erythropoiesis").

**Each subsection follows the same four-beat cycle**, often repeated 2–3 times within it:
1. **Motivation / bridge** — one or two sentences linking to what came before or to the literature: "The preceding results suggest that H1 may cooperate with Suv39h1/2 and SETDB1…"; "Several reports indicate that CpG methylation throughout the genome correlates with the presence of unmethylated H3K4 (6–9)… Therefore, we studied…"
2. **Approach** — stated as purpose + method, in one sentence: "To determine whether H1-mediated silencing of these repetitive sequences involves H3K9 methylation, we performed ChIP-qPCR for H3K9me3 at these loci in H1-low, H1d restored, and WT mESC lines."
3. **Observation** — what was seen, with the figure call and, where available, the number: "Strikingly, we observed a profound, 100-fold de-repression of major satellite transcripts in these highly H1-depleted (H1-low) mESCs (Fig. 2A)."
4. **Interpretation** — a sentence beginning "Thus," / "These results indicate that" / "Together, these results…" that states what the observation means, calibrated to the strength of evidence.

The first sentence of a subsection never restates the heading; it sets up the question. The last sentence of a subsection is always an interpretation, never an observation.

**Forward and backward references hold the argument together.** Use "The preceding results…", "As mentioned above,", "As described above,", "(see below)", "described below", "Consistent with the in vivo results (Fig. 2D) and with the results of the H1–DNMT protein interaction studies described below". Cross-cite earlier figures when a later experiment confirms them: "Combined with the in vivo and in vitro ChIP data (Figs. 2 B and C and 3B), these results indicate…".

**Explicitly rule out the trivial explanation before moving on.** This is the single most characteristic move:
- "Note that the levels of DNMT1 and DNMT3B are very similar in the three types of ES cells (Fig. S3), indicating that the observed changes in DNMT1 and DNMT3B occupancy at the two ICRs are not caused by changes in the levels of expression of the proteins."
- "This interaction is unlikely to be due to bridging by contaminating nucleic acids, because we found that interaction between GST-H1d and Suv39h1 was unaffected by inclusion of ethidium bromide…"
- "The absence of binding between Su(var)3-9 and another histone protein (GST-H2A) shows that interaction of Su(var)3-9 with H1 is not due to the high net positive charge of H1 or to a bridging artifact owing to contaminating nucleic acids."
- "Although the observed URE accessibility in purified BFUe could be due to small numbers of contaminating MPP, CMP, or GMP, it is unlikely because the total nonerythroid cell contamination level is estimated to be only 2.5% (28), which could not produce the level of accessibility we observed."

**Invoke the lab's prior work as a premise, not a boast.** "We previously reported that…", "In a previous study from our laboratory, we did not detect…", "Previous results from our laboratory showed that Drosophila H1 physically and functionally interacts with Su(var)3-9…", "consistent with our previous results demonstrating inhibition of SET7/9 by H1d (9)".

**Acknowledge what was not resolved, in one clause, and move on.** "a phenomenon that needs to be further explored"; "These latter results suggest the operation of alternative, possibly redundant H1-mediated silencing pathways at these loci (see below)."; "Although further studies are needed to understand how E2Fs may cooperate with PU.1…, these results support the view that…".

## 2. Sentence-level rules

- Average 22–25 words; ~10% of sentences run past 40 words and those are the ones that chain observation → qualifier → figure. Short declaratives (8–12 words) are reserved for conclusions: "Thus, the repressive function of H1 in vivo is directed particularly toward TEs."
- First person plural for decisions and findings ("we performed", "we found that", "we sought to"), passive for procedures ("qChIP was performed", "Transcript levels were compared", "chromatin was reconstituted"). Roughly 3:1 active:passive. Never "the authors".
- Tense: past for what was done and seen; present for what is true of the biology or the data ("H1 is enriched…", "Type 2 chromatin … exhibits strong nucleosome-repeat patterns"); present perfect for the literature ("has been shown to").
- One finding per sentence. When two observations must share a sentence, join them with "whereas" or "but" to make the contrast the point: "whereas H3K27me3 decreases with H1 depletion in all types of chromatin, H3K36me2 increases in type 3 chromatin and decreases in type 2 chromatin."
- Parenthetical figure calls sit at the end of the clause they support, not the sentence, when a sentence covers two panels: "…in WT ES cells, in which H1d also is readily detectable in these regions (Fig. 2A), and they were much reduced at these positions in the H1 TKO cells lacking H1d (Fig. 2 B and C)."
- Numbers are given with the comparator in parentheses or an explicit ratio: "(1,021 (B to A) versus 283 (A to B))", "4,406 increased vs 569 decreased; adjusted P < 0.05", "75% (235/313)", "from 15-fold to as much as 800-fold", "a 15-bp reduction in nucleosome repeat length". Fold changes under ten are words ("twofold", "threefold", "fourfold"); above are numerals ("100-fold").
- Spell out approximations with "~" or "approximately"; use "about 50%", "nearly all", "almost exclusively", "the vast majority", "very few", "only a modest".
- No bullet points, no numbered lists, no bold, no rhetorical questions, no exclamation marks. (The one exception: the Discussion of the Nature paper enumerates "(1)…(2)…(3)" inside a sentence.)

## 3. The lexicon

**Hedging ladder (use deliberately, in increasing order of commitment):**
"raise the possibility that" < "suggest that" / "support the view that" < "is consistent with" < "indicate that" (the default; ~105 uses) < "reveal" / "show" < "demonstrate" (rare, ~20 uses; reserved for direct in vitro proof or genetic sufficiency) < "establish" (once per paper, usually in the abstract).

**Sentence openers, in order of frequency:** "Thus," > "However," > "In contrast," > "Furthermore," > "Interestingly," > "Indeed," > "Therefore," > "Moreover," > "Importantly," > "Strikingly," > "Notably," > "Surprisingly," > "Remarkably,". "Indeed," introduces the confirming experiment after a prediction ("If H1 and Su(var)3-9 cooperate to silence TEs, then overexpression of Su(var)3-9 might ameliorate the effects of H1 depletion. Indeed, overexpression of Su(var)3-9 partially reverses…"). "Interestingly," flags an unexpected side observation that will not be pursued. "Importantly," precedes the control that makes the main result interpretable.

**Approach openers:** "To determine whether…", "To investigate this possibility, we…", "To test this prediction, we…", "To begin to understand the mechanism(s) by which…", "To further explore…", "To confirm that…", "To this end, we…", "We next sought to determine…", "We next asked if…", "it was of interest to determine whether…".

**Observation verbs:** "we found that", "we observed", "we detected", "revealed", "showed", "exhibited", "displayed". Intensifiers are few and fixed: "markedly", "strongly", "greatly", "readily", "substantially", "profound", "robust", "very similar", "nearly identical", "indistinguishable from", "largely unaffected", "mostly unaffected", "much reduced", "only very weakly if at all".

**Interpretation closers:** "These results indicate that…", "These results suggest that…", "These observations suggest that…", "Taken together, these results…", "Together, these results indicate that…", "Collectively, these results show that…", "We conclude that…", "These results provide…", "These findings reveal that…", "This conclusion is supported by…", "The results of these in vitro studies with purified components suggest that…".

**Characteristic phrases to reuse:** "entirely consistent with", "is readily detectable", "to a lesser extent", "albeit", "at least in part", "in a dose-dependent manner", "in the context of", "with respect to", "as far as we are aware", "it should be noted that", "Note that", "As expected,", "as evidenced by", "concomitant with", "accompanied by", "a surrogate for", "necessary and sufficient", "dispensable for", "required for".

**Words Art does not use in Results:** novel, elucidate, unravel, shed light, crucial/pivotal (except in Discussion), robustly (he uses "robust"), significantly as a synonym for "substantially" (he reserves it for statistics), "we hypothesized" (once in 13 papers; he writes "we reasoned that" or "The preceding results suggest that… To investigate this possibility"), "utilize" is rare — prefer "use". No "In this section", no "As shown in Figure 2", no "Figure 2 shows" except for a montage comparison ("Figure 3B shows a comparison of polytene chromosomes prepared from control and H1-depleted salivary glands").

## 4. Nomenclature and formatting conventions (house style)

- Linker histone subtypes: mouse H1a–H1e, H1(0); genes italic *H1c*, *H1d*, *H1e* (or *Hist1h1c* etc. when the journal requires); "H1 TKO", "H1c/d/e TKO", "H1-low", "H1cTKO;Vav-iCre", "H1d-restored", "H1 rescue lines". Drosophila "H1", "H1 KD", "H1-depleted".
- Cells: mESCs, ES-EP, FL-EP, MEL cells, BFUe, CFUe, CMP, KSL. Always define once at first use and then use the abbreviation.
- Modifications: H3K9me2/H3K9me3 (mouse papers) or H3K9Me2 (fly papers, journal-dependent) — be consistent within a manuscript. H3K27me3, H3K36me2, H3K4me3.
- Methods: qChIP, ChIP-qPCR, ChIP-seq (or ChIP-Seq in 2011–2012), RT-qPCR/qRT-PCR, RP-HPLC, MNase, NRL, ATAC-seq, CUT&Tag, GST pull-down, "in vitro ChIP", "chromatosome stop assay".
- Figure citations follow the journal exactly: PNAS "(Fig. 2 B and C)", "(SI Appendix, Fig. S2 A and B)"; Nature "(Fig. 2a, Extended Data Fig. 5a, b)"; Genes Dev "(Fig. 3B; Supplemental Fig. 4)"; Science "(fig. S2, A and B)" and "(Fig. 1, B and D)"; JBC "(Fig. 2, A and B)"; PLoS "(Figure 4C and 4D)". Tables: "(Table 1)".
- Statistics inline only when they carry the argument: "(P value <2.2 × 10−16)", "(r2 = 0.89, P = 3.93 × 10−9, unpaired, two-tailed Student's t-test)", "(adjusted P < 0.05)". Otherwise leave stats to the legend.
- Spelling follows the journal (Nature: haematopoietic, analysed, colour; PNAS/JBC: hematopoietic, analyzed). Default US.

## 5. Paragraph templates with exemplars

**Opening a subsection from prior data**
"The strong enrichment of H1 in regions of the genome that harbor repetitive sequences and are repressed via Suv39h1/2- and SETDB1-mediated H3K9 methylation suggests that H1 may be required for maintaining silencing of these regions. To investigate this possibility, we measured the transcript levels of several repetitive elements within the satellite, LINE, and LTR families… Transcript levels were compared in three clones of our previously described H1c/d/e TKO mESCs (8) and in WT control cells. For each of the repetitive elements assayed, we observed a mild increase in transcript level in some clones (Fig. 2 A and B)."

**Explaining a weak result and escalating the perturbation**
"One possible explanation for the relatively modest effects of H1 depletion on repetitive sequence transcripts in TKO mESCs is that the total H1 content in these cells is reduced by only 50% compared with WT, owing to compensatory expression of the remaining H1 subtypes, H1a and H1b (8). Therefore, we sought to further reduce H1 levels in the TKO mESCs by targeted deletion of Hist1h1a and Hist1h1b with CRISPR-Cas9."

**Necessity then sufficiency**
"To determine whether H1 is simply required for maintaining silencing of these elements or whether it can initiate silencing, we used CRISPR-Cas9–mediated homology-directed repair to reintroduce an H1d gene into the H1-low mESCs… We observed that expression of the exogenous H1d protein in these cells restored repression of each type of repetitive element (Fig. 2 A and B). These results suggest that H1 is capable of initiating silencing of these repetitive sequences."

**Domain mapping**
"We found that DNMT1 and DNMT3B bound to the full-length fusion protein and to the fusion protein containing the last 70 residues of the CTD but did not bind appreciably to the fusion protein containing residues 1–151 (Fig. 4F). The importance of the histone H1 CTD for binding DNMT1 and DNMT3B was demonstrated further by showing that substituting this region from H1d for the corresponding region in H1c, which binds these DNMTs only very weakly if at all (Fig. 4 A, D, and E), greatly increases binding of the two DNMTs (Fig. S4B). These results indicate that the last 70 residues of the H1d CTD are necessary and sufficient to bind both DNMT1 and DNMT3B."

**Prediction → test → Indeed**
"If H1 and Su(var)3-9 cooperate to silence TEs, then overexpression of Su(var)3-9 might ameliorate the effects of H1 depletion. Indeed, overexpression of Su(var)3-9 partially reverses the activation of TE and Het Ste expression accompanying H1 depletion in SGs (Fig. 2D) and larvae (fig. S2F). In addition, Su(var)3-9 overexpression partially restores the decreased viability of flies caused by H1 depletion (table S3). Conversely, Su(var)3-9 mutation strongly enhances the lethality caused by even moderate H1 depletion (table S3)."

**Genome-wide description with a defined vocabulary**
"…revealed three distinct patterns of chromatin architectural changes, which we defined as types 1, 2 and 3 chromatin according to their response to H1 depletion (Fig. 2g, Extended Data Fig. 5h). Type 1 chromatin, encompassing 3% of the genome consisting mostly of 'open', nucleosome-free DNA in promoters and enhancers, is largely unaffected by H1 depletion. Type 2 chromatin, encompassing 23% of the genome and consisting of gene bodies with various levels of transcription, exhibits strong nucleosome-repeat patterns and reductions in nucleosome-repeat length (NRL) in H1-depleted CD8+ T cells."

**Closing a subsection with the synthesis**
"Taken together, studies of pericentric chromatin structure and the effects of curaxin on satellite transcription support the view that the much larger de-repression of major satellite transcription in H1-low compared with Suv39h1/2 double-null mESCs is due to the combined effects of reduced H1-mediated chromatin compaction and H1-stimulated Suv39h1/2-mediated deposition of H3K9 methylation at these loci."

## 6. Checklist before returning Results text

1. Every heading is a declarative finding.
2. Every paragraph ends on an interpretation sentence; no paragraph ends on a figure call.
3. Every new experiment is introduced by its purpose ("To determine whether…") or by "The preceding results suggest…".
4. The obvious artifact/trivial explanation for each key result has been addressed in one sentence.
5. Verbs of commitment match the evidence (suggest / indicate / demonstrate).
6. Comparators and numbers are explicit; fold changes and percentages have their denominators.
7. Figure calls use the target journal's exact format and sit at the clause they support.
8. No lists, no bold, no "novel", no "elucidate", no "we hypothesized" (use "we reasoned" / "To investigate this possibility").
9. Prior lab work is cited as a premise in the first or second sentence where relevant.
10. The subsection's final sentence could stand alone as the figure title.

Companion skill: `art_legends_writing` for figure legends; keep nomenclature, abbreviations and figure-panel references identical between the two.
